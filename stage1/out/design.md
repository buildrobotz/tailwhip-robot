## 2. Balance

- Body (everything but the wheels and shaft) 306 g, centre of mass **+0.1 mm** fore-aft, -0.8 mm sideways and **85.3 mm above the axle**; wheels and shaft 45 g.
- Body pitch inertia about the axle 3.46e-03 kg m2. Fall time constant sqrt(I / m g h) = **116 ms**: a lean roughly doubles every 80 ms.
- Torque to hold a lean: m g h sin(th) = 0.256 sin(th) N m. One TT motor stalls at about 0.08 N m at 6 V [E]; the tyres slip at mu m g r = 0.090 N m (mu 0.8). Steady lean one motor can hold: **18 deg**.
- Track 52.0 mm (wheel centres). Centre of mass 107 mm above the floor, so it tips sideways past **20 deg** of roll. Flat floors only.

## 3. Falls

- From the meshes: falling forward it first touches at **24.7 deg** at (82, 0, 2) mm; falling back at **24.7 deg** at (-82, 0, 2) mm. The crash bar formula gives 24.7 deg.
- At rest the centre of mass is 36 mm out and the bar 73 mm out, so it stays there.
- Getting back up needs the axle to accelerate at g tan(th) = 4.5 m/s2 (tyre mu above 0.46).

## 6. Robustness: falls

Printed PLA [E]: E = 2300 MPa, strength 40 MPa along the layers and 20 MPa across them. Model: the body pivots on a fixed axle (the wheels cannot roll away, which makes it worse than real), lands on the crash bar through the bending stiffness of the arm (rigid floor), and the battery, tray and ESP32 ride on the mast's own bending spring. Peak forces come from integrating that through the impact; stress = F L c / I on sections sliced from the meshes. Two falls: a topple from upright (released at rest), and a hard fall (the same after a 3 rad/s shove, about twice the largest shove in the simulator).

- Body angular speed when the bar lands: 3.68 rad/s (topple), 4.75 rad/s (hard). Body energy 23 / 39 mJ.

- The mast carries 155 g (tray, holder, cells, strap, switch, ESP32, BMS, buck, wires) with its centre at z 135 mm. Bending stiffness, sliced: z 76: I 13419 mm4, z 98: I 3505 mm4, z 110: I 2719 mm4. The old 3 mm plate was 140 mm4.
- First bending mode of the loaded mast, rough: 281 Hz (the old plate, same load: 28 Hz). Far from the 4 Hz wobble.
- Mast to core: the hard-fall mast force 71 N acts 59 mm above the foot, 4153 N mm. Over the 32 mm between the screw rows that is 65 N per M3 self-tapper, against a few hundred N to pull one out of 8 mm of PLA [E].
- The same force on the tray and holder: 71 N, carried by the tray floor bearing on the mast top and the two M3 screws in shear. A side fall onto the tray is the larger unknown: drop-test it on carpet first.
- Cheeks (2.3 mm): a sideways landing on one wheel (0.34 kg at 30 g, 100 N [E]) pushes the gearbox into the far cheek, which is backed by the whole gearbox face: under 0.2 MPa in bearing, and 0.36 MPa sheared round the 121 mm pocket edge. Pressing a wheel on (50 N [E]) is smaller still. The cheek is not a strength problem; its risk is print quality, because it prints as a bridge (section 5).
